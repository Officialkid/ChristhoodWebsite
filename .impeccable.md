# Christhood Ministries website: design brief

Source: the commissioned redesign proposal (`docs/Christhood_Website_Redesign_Proposal.docx`) and the owner's direction of 1 Nov 2026. Edit this file if any of it is wrong.

## Design Context

### Users
- A Saturday congregation and their families in Nairobi and across Kenya, mostly on mid-range Android phones with mobile data.
- First-time visitors who need the time, place and what to expect, within seconds.
- Parents deciding on a camp or mentorship programme for a child or teenager, who need dates, cost and a way to register.
- Young people (13 to 25), church members, volunteers, donors and the team who keep the site current.
- Jobs to do: plan a visit, register for a camp, find a ministry, read what the church believes, give, ask for prayer or counselling.

### Brand personality
- Three words: **communal, earnest, grounded**.
- Voice: plain and warm, spoken the way a leader speaks to a family. No marketing language, no theology jargon on the surface.
- Colours are fixed by the brand: royal purple and gold (blue exists in the brand but is not used in the interface).

### Aesthetic direction
- **Visual thesis:** a printed order-of-service booklet turned into a website. Warm paper, ink-dark type, one royal purple, gold used like a highlighter. Photographs do the decorating.
- Light theme by default (read in daylight by families on phones), with solid purple bands for emphasis.
- Type: Archivo (a sturdy grotesque with a width axis) for headings, navigation and labels; Literata (a text serif made for reading on screens) for long passages.
- Anti-references (explicitly NOT): gradient glows, glassmorphism, gradient or italic-gold headings, rounded cards with shadows around everything, icon tiles, stat strips, tracked all-caps labels, scrolling ribbons, pill buttons. Anything that reads as "AI made this".
- References for structure (borrow patterns, not looks): the church benchmarks in the proposal (Elevation, Life.Church, Passion City), editorial magazine layouts.

### Design principles
1. **Photographs first.** Real photos of this congregation are the decoration. Never put a gradient or card over them. Keep text off busy image areas.
2. **Cardless by default.** Use columns, hairline rules, lists and media blocks. A box is allowed only when it is a form or an action.
3. **One accent.** Purple is the brand and the large surfaces. Gold marks the single main action on a screen and nothing else.
4. **Every section has one job** and one dominant idea. Cut copy until it is only what a visitor needs.
5. **Plain words, large type, high contrast.** Body text at least 17px, 65 characters per line, WCAG AA everywhere, touch targets at least 44px.
6. **Motion has a purpose.** Allowed: the hero headline revealing line by line (GSAP), slow dust in the hero light (React Three Fiber, hard-edged specks, never glows), a slow drift on the hero photo, the weekly verse as a short looping film (Remotion) with a visible pause button, pictures uncovering as they scroll in, lists rising in turn, and a photo preview on the ministry list. All of it is switched off for reduced motion, low-power devices and Save-Data, and none of it may sit between a visitor and the content.

### Team area (`/123admin`)
- It is an application, not a brochure: utility words ("Events", "New guests"), a calm purple sidebar, ruled lists instead of card mosaics, one gold button per screen for the main action, an icon on every menu entry.
- Written for non-technical department heads. No jargon. Every screen says what it changes and what the public will see.
- Works one-handed on a phone and on a folded device: the sidebar becomes a drawer below 1024px, all controls are at least 44px.
- **How the team area works now (2 Nov 2026):** one vocabulary on every screen. A status is a small mark and a word (`Status` in `src/components/admin/ui.tsx`), never a coloured block. Filters are underlined choices (`.a-tabs`). Long lists are real tables (`DataTable`) that stack into two short lines on a phone; a cell marked `k` is the one that stays on the first line. Long forms keep their Save button in view (`.a-bar`). Numbers are tabular. The overview is a desk, not a menu: what is waiting, the camps and how each stands, today at camp when one is on, and recent changes; the full list of screens sits at the bottom. The search bar (Ctrl K) opens screens, quick actions, and the people and applications that person may already see. No 3D, no decoration: this is where people work.
